Question 1 — The four layers of the Earth (4 marks)

1
From the outside in: crust → mantle → outer core → inner core.
1 mark each. The thin outer shell is the crust, then the thick mantle, then the liquid outer core, and the solid inner core at the centre.

Question 2 — Three rocks A, B, C (4 marks)

2a
Rock A = sedimentary · Rock B = igneous · Rock C = metamorphic.
2 marks. A is formed in layers and holds fossils (sedimentary); B forms when molten rock cools and has crystals (igneous); C is very hard with fossils distorted by heat and pressure (metamorphic).
2b
Small particles (sediment) are deposited in layers — usually at the bottom of a sea or lake — and over millions of years the layers are compressed (squashed) and cemented together into rock.
2 marks — 1 for sediment settling/being deposited in layers, 1 for it being compressed/cemented over a long time.

Question 4 — Helen soaks rocks in water (8 marks)

4a
The sandstone's mass increased (25.5 → 27.6 g) because water soaked into its spaces, while granite and marble stayed the same mass — so only sandstone has pores.
1 mark for using the mass change as the evidence.
4b i
The freezing water expands and forces the spaces apart, so the sandstone cracks / breaks up.
1 mark.
4b ii
Freeze–thaw weathering.
1 mark. (Accept "freeze-thaw" / physical weathering.)
4c
City rain is acidic (acid rain). Granite does not react with acid, so it won't be worn away — the statue lasts longer. Marble and sandstone react with acid and would be eroded/damaged.
1 mark for linking granite's lack of reaction with acid to it surviving acidic city air/rain.
4d i
granite → igneous · marble → metamorphic · sandstone → sedimentary.
2 marks.
4d ii
sedimentary → particles are deposited in layers · igneous → formed when magma cools · metamorphic → the effect of high temperature and pressure on limestone.
2 marks.

Question 5 — Section through a volcano (5 marks)

5a
Underground the magma is surrounded by hot rock and insulated, so it cools very slowly; lava on the surface is exposed to the cool air and loses heat quickly, so it sets fast.
1 mark.

Because the magma cools slowly underground, giving the crystals more time to grow big. (Lava cools quickly, so its crystals are small.)

5c ii
Marble.

Question 6 — Polar bears & the shrinking ice (6 marks)

6a i
The extent of the ice platform is decreasing (getting smaller) over time.
1 mark. It falls steadily from 3.5 km in 1950 to 2.2 km in 2010.
6a ii
Burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide (a greenhouse gas). Greenhouse gases trap heat in the atmosphere (the greenhouse effect), so the Earth warms and ice melts. The table is evidence because the ice platform has steadily shrunk from 1950 to 2010, just as you'd expect with warming.
3 marks — 1 for CO₂ from burning fossil fuels, 1 for greenhouse gases trapping heat / warming, 1 for the shrinking ice being the supporting evidence.
6b i
Ice thinner than 20 cm is too weak to hold a bear's weight — it would crack and the bear would fall through, so bears keep off it.
1 mark.
6b ii
There will be less ice to support them, so the number of polar bears will fall / decline (harder to hunt seals, so they may starve — numbers could drop towards extinction).
1 mark.

Question 8 — Tectonic plates (5 marks)

8a i
The crust.
1 mark.
8a ii
The plates (crust) are less dense than the mantle beneath, and the mantle behaves like a thick liquid — so the solid plates float on top.
1 mark.
8b i
The Earth's core — the source of heat at the centre of the Earth.
1 mark. The candle heats the oil just as the core heats the mantle.
8b ii
Prediction: the biscuit pieces slowly move (drift) across the surface of the oil.
Explanation: the candle heats the oil, setting up convection currents — hot oil rises, cools and sinks — and these currents carry the biscuits, just as convection currents in the mantle move the tectonic plates.
2 marks — 1 for predicting the pieces move, 1 for explaining it with convection currents in the heated oil (modelling the mantle).
