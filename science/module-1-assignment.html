Note on the diagram questions
Several parts (Q1a, Q2a, Q2b, Q4a) are "draw a line to match" or "fill in the table" tasks on the question paper. The correct pairings are given below as the answer; on paper they are simply drawn as joining lines or written into the boxes.

Question 1 — The digestive system (7 marks)

1a
A = stomach → digests protein (given) · B = large intestine → absorbs water · C = pancreas → produces enzymes · D = small intestine → absorbs digested food
3 marks (the A line is already drawn for you). Each remaining letter joins to one organ and one function:
B (large intestine) absorbs water from the remaining food.
C (pancreas) produces digestive enzymes.
D (small intestine) is where digested food is absorbed into the blood.
1b
To provide energy
Glucose is a sugar (a carbohydrate), and carbohydrates are the body's main energy source — handy for a quick energy boost before a race.
1c i
Clare
Lemonade and a jam doughnut are both very high in sugar.
1c ii
Nadia
A cheeseburger and chips are both high in fat.
1c iii
It can lead to obesity and heart disease.
1 mark for any sensible reason: too much fat causes weight gain / obesity, raises cholesterol, and can block the arteries leading to heart disease.

Question 2 — Café card & nutrients (6 marks)

2a
Least fat: single burger + medium cola (or orange juice) + baked potato. Most fibre: double burger + any drink + baked potato.
2 marks. Read the values off the card:
Least fat — single burger (17 g, lowest), cola or orange juice (0 g), baked potato (0 g).
Most fibre — double burger (1.2 g, highest), the drinks contain no fibre, baked potato (9 g, far more than fries' 3 g).
2b
calcium → for healthy teeth and bones · fibre → to keep the intestine working properly · protein → for growth and repair · sugar → to provide energy
4 marks (one per correct line). "For insulation" is the odd one out (that's the job of fat) and is left unmatched.

Question 3 — Cells in the windpipe (9 marks)

3a i
A tissue
A group of similar cells working together is a tissue.
3a ii
The sticky mucus traps bacteria and dust; the cilia beat to sweep the mucus up and out of the lungs.
2 marks: 1 for the mucus trapping particles, 1 for the cilia sweeping the mucus up the windpipe (towards the throat, to be swallowed or coughed out).
3b
Mucus builds up because the damaged cilia can no longer sweep it away, so more dust and bacteria reach the lungs — leading to a cough and more chest infections.
2 marks for any two linked consequences: mucus is not cleared → builds up → "smoker's cough"; trapped bacteria → more infections / chest disease.
3c i
Tar — a carcinogen that causes lung cancer (and damages the cilia).
2 marks: 1 for naming tar, 1 for a correct harmful effect.
3c ii
Carbon monoxide — binds to red blood cells so the blood carries less oxygen.
2 marks: 1 for naming the substance, 1 for the effect. Nicotine (addictive, raises heart rate and blood pressure) is also accepted.

Question 4 — Unbalanced diets (5 marks)

4a
too much fat → heart disease · too much sugar → tooth decay · not enough protein → poor growth · not enough carbohydrate → not much energy
4 marks, one per correct line.
4b
Chips are very high in fat, so eating a lot every day can cause obesity and heart disease — and a diet of mostly chips lacks other nutrients.
1 mark for linking the high fat content to a health problem (obesity / heart disease), or for noting it is an unbalanced diet missing vitamins, fibre and protein.

Question 5 — Smoking & heart disease (5 marks)

5a
1. The more cigarettes smoked per day, the higher the % of deaths from heart disease. 2. For the same number of cigarettes, older men have a higher % of deaths than younger men.
2 marks, one per valid conclusion drawn from the bars: a positive link between cigarettes and heart-disease deaths, and that the death rate rises with age group (55–64 highest).
5b
Fat narrows the arteries, reducing blood flow to the heart muscle, so it receives less oxygen and glucose and cannot respire enough to keep beating properly (risking a heart attack).
3 marks: 1 fat narrows / blocks the artery; 1 less blood / oxygen reaches the heart muscle; 1 the muscle cannot respire / work properly (→ heart attack).

Question 6 — Digesting jelly experiment (7 marks)

6a i
Enzymes
Enzymes are the substances that speed up (catalyse) digestion.
6a ii
It is broken down into smaller molecules (amino acids).
Digestion breaks the large protein molecule into small, soluble amino acids that can be absorbed.
6b i
It is the control — with cold water and no pineapple juice it shows the jelly does not digest on its own, for comparison.
Tube A has no pineapple juice (no enzyme), so it shows what happens without the enzyme — the fair comparison for B and C.
6b ii
The chopped jelly in C digested faster (1 hour vs 2 hours) — chopping increases the surface area, just as chewing does.
Smaller pieces give a larger surface area for the enzyme to act on, so digestion is quicker. Chewing food has the same effect.
6c i
Test tube D = a 5 g jelly cube chopped into pieces + 15 cm³ of pineapple juice that has been boiled (then cooled) — everything else the same as C.
2 marks: 1 for the juice being boiled, 1 for keeping the rest identical to tube C (chopped jelly, same amounts) so only the boiling is being tested.
6c ii
The jelly would not be digested (still there) after 2 hours.
Boiling destroys (denatures) the enzyme, so it can no longer digest the protein.
