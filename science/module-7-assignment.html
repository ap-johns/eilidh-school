Question 1 — Twins Sara & Helen (6 marks)

1a i
Genetic information is carried by genes (found on chromosomes in the nucleus). It is passed to the child through the sex cells / gametes — the egg and the sperm — which join at fertilisation.
2 marks — 1 for genes/chromosomes carrying the information, 1 for it being passed on through the egg and sperm (gametes) at fertilisation.
1a ii
Because they inherit genes from both parents — roughly half from the mother's egg and half from the father's sperm — so they show a mix of both parents' features.
1 mark.
1b
Identical twins come from one fertilised egg that split in two, so they have exactly the same genes (the same genetic information).
1 mark.
1c
Eye colour is controlled only by genes, and the twins' genes are identical, so their eye colour is the same. Weight and skin colour are also affected by the environment — Sara works outdoors in a hot country (so her skin tans darker) and their diets/lifestyles differ (so their weights differ).
2 marks — 1 for eye colour being determined by genes alone, 1 for weight/skin colour being affected by the environment (which is different for each twin).

Question 7 — Fish population graph (5 marks)

7a
B — the curve is steepest there, so the population is rising fastest.
1 mark. The steeper the line, the quicker the population is increasing.
7b
C — the curve starts to level off (growth slows down), showing food and space are running short so the fish begin to compete.
1 mark. As competition sets in, the rate of increase falls, bending the curve towards flat.
7c
The birth rate and death rate are equal (balanced), so the population stays constant. You can tell because the line is flat / horizontal.
1 mark. A level line means the number is not changing, so births are balancing deaths.
7d
Any two, e.g. a disease/epidemic spread through the fish; not enough food (starvation); a new predator arrived; or the water became polluted / low in oxygen.
2 marks — 1 per sensible reason for a sudden population crash.
