Handy formulae & drawn parts

speed = distance ÷ time
moment = force × distance
pressure = force ÷ area

Question 1a (matching lines) and Question 6c i (drawing the walker's line) are drawn by hand; Q6 values are read off the graph, so those are approximate.

Question 1 — Snow-buggy pulled by a sail (7 marks)

1a
weight of the buggy → B · force pulling the buggy along → A · friction between the skis and the snow → C
3 marks (1 per line). Weight always acts straight down (B). The sail pulls the buggy along in its direction of travel (A). Friction opposes the motion, so it points the opposite way (C). (D, pointing up, is the reaction from the snow — not in the list.)
1b
800 km
80 km × 10 days = 800 km.
1c
At the start the total mass was greater (295 kg vs 130 kg), so its weight pressed down harder — more pressure on the snow, so it sank deeper.
1 mark. As the food and equipment were used up, the mass (and so the weight and the pressure) fell, so it sank less by the end.
1d
Skis have a larger area in contact with the snow, so they spread the weight out — less pressure, so the buggy doesn't sink in.
1 mark. Thin wheels have a small contact area → high pressure → they sink into the snow.
1e
A bigger sail has a larger area to catch the wind, so it collects more wind and gives a bigger forward (pushing) force.
1 mark. Greater forward force → faster.

Question 2 — Balancing mobile (5 marks)

Elephant 10 cm left of X (weight 0.2 N); monkey 20 cm right of X.

2a i
2 N cm
moment = force × distance = 0.2 N × 10 cm = 2 N cm. (Or 0.02 N m if you convert 10 cm to 0.1 m.)
2a ii
2 N cm
The mobile balances, so the monkey's moment must equal the elephant's moment.
2a iii
0.1 N
moment = force × distance, so 2 = W × 20 → W = 2 ÷ 20 = 0.1 N.
2b
0.3 N
String A holds up the whole mobile, so the tension equals the total weight hanging: 0.2 + 0.1 = 0.3 N.

Question 3 — Megan's bike time-trial (5 marks)

3a i
12.5 m/s
speed = distance ÷ time = 400 ÷ 32 = 12.5 m/s.
3a ii
They are equal (balanced).
1 mark. At a constant speed there is no resultant force, so the forward force equals the backward force.
3b i
The forward force is now greater than the backward force.
1 mark.
3b ii
Being streamlined reduces air resistance, so the backward force drops. With the same pedal force but less drag there is a resultant forward force, so she speeds up.
1 mark.

Question 4 — Falling mass splits logs (6 marks)

4a
From chemical energy in his muscles to gravitational potential energy.
1 mark.
4b i
50 J
1 mark. At most all of the 50 J of GPE can become kinetic energy (energy is conserved).
4b ii
Some energy is lost to friction (between the mass and the rod) and air resistance, transferred as heat.
1 mark.
4c
1. Lift/drop the mass from a greater height (more GPE).   2. Use a heavier mass.
2 marks. (Also acceptable: reduce the friction on the rod.)
4d
Blade A — it tapers to the sharper point, so it has the smaller area in contact with the log.
1 mark. pressure = force ÷ area: the two blades have the same weight (force), so the one with the smaller contact area (the sharper point, A) gives the greater pressure.

Question 5 — Builder removing a post (5 marks)

5a i
240 N m
moment = force × distance = 300 N × 0.8 m = 240 N m (distance measured from the pivot P).
5a ii
150 N
Same moment, but now 1.6 m from the pivot: F × 1.6 = 240 → F = 240 ÷ 1.6 = 150 N. (Further from the pivot, so less force is needed.)
5b
600 N/m² (600 Pa)
pressure = force ÷ area = 120 ÷ 0.2 = 600 N/m².

Question 6 — Cyclist vs runner distance–time graph (6 marks)

6a i
≈ 9 s
1 mark. Read where the cyclist's curve levels off at 100 m (about 9 seconds).
6a ii
≈ 30 m
1 mark. At the time the cyclist reaches 100 m (~9 s), the runner is at about 70 m, so roughly 100 − 70 = 30 m behind.
6a iii
≈ 4 s
1 mark. The runner reaches 100 m at about 13 s and the cyclist at about 9 s, so the runner takes roughly 13 − 9 = 4 s longer.
6b
The line is straight between 3 s and 6 s.
1 mark. A straight (constant-gradient) section of a distance–time graph means a steady speed.
6c i · line
A straight line from (0 s, 0 m) to (15 s, 30 m).
1 mark. At 2 m/s the walker covers 2 × 15 = 30 m in 15 s, so rule a straight line up to the point (15, 30).
6c ii
50 s
time = distance ÷ speed = 100 ÷ 2 = 50 s.

Question 7 — Mia the dancer & pressure (4 marks)

7a
1.25 N/cm²
pressure = force ÷ area = 500 ÷ 400 = 1.25 N/cm².
7b
The pressure increases (a lot).
1 mark. The same 500 N now acts on a much smaller area (10 cm² instead of 400 cm²), so the pressure is much greater — 500 ÷ 10 = 50 N/cm², 40 times bigger.
7c
All her weight acts on a tiny area, so the pressure on her toes is very high — enough to injure them.
1 mark.
