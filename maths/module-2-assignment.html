Wolsey Hall LS Maths Assignment Two (Decimals & linear graphs) — worked answers for all 40 marks. This is a non-calculator assignment; several parts specify a particular method (partitioning, column, short division), so the answer alone won't score full marks — the stated method must be shown.

Question 1 — Column method (2 marks)

1
88
Keep the decimal points lined up.
Add first: 61.4 + 43.8 = 105.2 (the .4 + .8 = 1.2, write .2 and carry 1).
Then subtract: 105.2 − 17.2 = 88.0 = 88.

Question 2 — Addition by partitioning (4 marks)

Split each number into parts, add the matching parts, then recombine. (No marks for the column method here.)

2a
62
47 + 15 = (40 + 10) + (7 + 5) = 50 + 12 = 62.
2b
18.2
13.4 + 4.8 = (13 + 4) + (0.4 + 0.8) = 17 + 1.2 = 18.2.

Question 3 — Subtraction by partitioning (4 marks)

Take away one part at a time. (No marks for the column method here.)

3a
47
82 − 35: take away 30 then 5.
82 − 30 = 52; 52 − 5 = 47.
3b
1.4
3.1 − 1.7: take away 1 then 0.7.
3.1 − 1 = 2.1; 2.1 − 0.7 = 1.4.

Question 4 — Estimate, then column multiply (2 marks)

4
Estimate ≈ 48; exact answer 47.2
Estimate by rounding 2.95 to 3: 16 × 3 = 48.
Exact: ignore the decimal and multiply 16 × 295 = 4720, then put the two decimal places back: 4720 ÷ 100 = 47.2.
47.2 is close to the estimate of 48, so it looks right.

Question 5 — Dividing by a decimal (4 marks)

Multiply both numbers by 10 (or 100) so you are dividing by a whole number — this doesn't change the answer.

5a
65
26 ÷ 0.4: multiply both by 10 → 260 ÷ 4 = 65.
5b
80
7.2 ÷ 0.09: multiply both by 100 → 720 ÷ 9 = 80.

Question 6 — Fractions to decimals by short division (4 marks)

A fraction is just the top divided by the bottom. Divide, adding zeros after the decimal point as needed.

6a
7/8 = 0.875
7 ÷ 8 using 7.000:
70 ÷ 8 = 8 r 6; 60 ÷ 8 = 7 r 4; 40 ÷ 8 = 5 r 0.
Reading off: 0.875.
6b
5/12 = 0.41666… = 0.416̇ (the 6 recurs)
5 ÷ 12 using 5.00000:
50 ÷ 12 = 4 r 2; 20 ÷ 12 = 1 r 8; 80 ÷ 12 = 6 r 8; 80 ÷ 12 = 6 r 8 …
The remainder of 8 repeats forever, so the 6 recurs: 0.41666….

Question 7 — Graph of y = 2x + 1 (6 marks)

7a
y-values: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9
For each x, work out 2x + 1:
x = 0 → 1; x = 1 → 3; x = 2 → 5; x = 3 → 7; x = 4 → 9.
7b
A straight line through (0, 1), (1, 3), (2, 5), (3, 7), (4, 9)
Plot the five points from the table and join them with a ruled straight line. It crosses the y-axis at 1 and rises 2 squares for every 1 across (the gradient is 2).

Question 8 — Miles to kilometres (12 marks)

Use km = miles × 1.6.

8a
Kilometres: 0, 8, 16, 32, 80
0 × 1.6 = 0; 5 × 1.6 = 8; 10 × 1.6 = 16; 20 × 1.6 = 32; 50 × 1.6 = 80.
8b
A straight line from (0, 0) through (50, 80)
Miles on the horizontal axis (0–50), kilometres on the vertical axis (0–80). Plot the five pairs from part a and join with a ruled straight line through the origin.
8c
48 km
From 30 on the miles axis, go straight up to the line, then across to the km axis — it reads 48.
Check with the formula: 30 × 1.6 = 48 km.

Question 9 — Distance–time graphs (2 marks)

On a distance–time graph: a steeper line means faster; a flat (horizontal) section means stopped; a line coming back down to the start means returning home.

9a
Graph 3
"Returns to the starting point, return faster than the outward journey" — the line goes up then comes back down to zero (graph 3), with the downward part steeper than the upward part.
9b
Graph 2
"Stopped to make a delivery, then carried on further away" — the line rises, goes flat (the stop), then rises again to a greater distance (graph 2).
