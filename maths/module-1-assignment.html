Question 1 — Ordering numbers (4 marks)

Smallest to largest. For negatives, the bigger the digit, the smaller the number.

1a
−17, −7, −2, 0, 9
All the negatives come first, most negative leading: −17 is the smallest, then −7, then −2, then 0, then the positive 9.
1b
−4.2, −1.7, −0.3, 0.9, 12.7
−4.2 is the most negative, then −1.7, then −0.3; the positives 0.9 and 12.7 follow.

Question 2 — Adding & subtracting negatives (4 marks)

Two signs together: − − becomes +; + + stays +; a single − stays −.

2a
−3
−5 − (−2) = −5 + 2 = −3 (the two minuses become a plus).
2b
−38
−17 + (−21) = −17 − 21 = −38 (adding a negative is subtracting).
2c
−4
3 − (+7) = 3 − 7 = −4.
2d
−12
−2 − 10 = −12 (start at −2 and go 10 more in the negative direction).

Question 3 — Multiplying & dividing negatives (4 marks)

Same signs → positive answer; different signs → negative answer.

3a
−32
8 × (−4): different signs → negative. 8 × 4 = 32, so −32.
3b
−5.5
−11 ÷ 2: different signs → negative. 11 ÷ 2 = 5.5, so −5.5.
3c
8
−72 ÷ (−9): same signs → positive. 72 ÷ 9 = 8.
3d
24
−4 × (−6): same signs → positive. 4 × 6 = 24.

Question 4 — Temperature (3 marks)

4a
−10 °C
Falls 6° from −4°: −4 − 6 = −10 °C.
4b
31 °C
Difference = inside − outside = 21 − (−10) = 21 + 10 = 31 °C.
(Or count up the number line from −10 to 21.)

Question 5 — Fibonacci-style sequences (3 marks)

Each term is the sum of the two terms before it. Use the known terms either side of the gap to check.

5a
25  →  5, 20, 25, 45, 70
5 + 20 = 25. Check: 20 + 25 = 45 ✓ and 25 + 45 = 70 ✓.
5b
1.0  →  0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 1.0, 1.6
0.4 + 0.6 = 1.0. Check: 0.6 + 1.0 = 1.6 ✓.
5c
−13  →  −5, −1, −6, −7, −13
Add the previous two: −6 + (−7) = −13. Check the pattern holds: −5 + (−1) = −6 ✓ and −1 + (−6) = −7 ✓.

Question 6 — Blue & white tile pattern (7 marks)

Each diagram is two rows. The top row is all blue; the bottom row is one blue tile, then the white tiles, then one blue tile. Diagram 1 has 1 white tile, diagram 2 has 2, and so on.

6a
Diagram 4: 8 blue + 4 white; Diagram 5: 9 blue + 5 white
The shape grows by one column each time. Diagram 4 = top row of 6 blue, bottom row blue · 4 white · blue. Diagram 5 = top row of 7 blue, bottom row blue · 5 white · blue.
6b
White: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5  ·  Blue: 5, 6, 7, 8, 9
For each diagram the white tiles count up 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and the blue tiles are always 4 more: 5, 6, 7, 8, 9.
6c
First term 5; add 1 each time (common difference +1).
The blue-tile sequence is 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 … — it starts at 5 and goes up by 1 every diagram.
6d
Blue tiles = position + 4   (nth term = n + 4)
Diagram 1 → 5, diagram 2 → 6, diagram 3 → 7. Each blue count is the diagram number plus 4, so the nth term is n + 4.

Question 7 — Flower bush (5 marks)

The bush starts with 5 buds already flowered, and 3 more flower each week.

7a
The 5 is the count at planting (week 0); by the end of week 1 three more have opened, giving 8.
We are counting the total flowered by the end of each week. After the first week: 5 + 3 = 8; after the second: 8 + 3 = 11; after the third: 14. The 5 itself happens before any week has passed, so it isn't the first term of the weekly sequence.
7b
14 buds
5 + 3 × 3 = 5 + 9 = 14.
7c
Buds after n weeks = 3n + 5
Start at 5, add 3 for each week, so after n weeks the total is 5 + 3n = 3n + 5.
7d
9 weeks
Set 3n + 5 = 32: 3n = 27, so n = 9 weeks.

Question 8 — Linear sequences (10 marks)

8a i
First term 16; common difference +5.
16, 21, 26, 31, 36 — each term is 5 more than the one before.
8a ii
First term 75; common difference −9.
75, 66, 57, 48, 39 — each term is 9 less than the one before.
8b i
nth term = 5n + 11
Common difference 5 → starts 5n. Adjust to hit the first term: 5 × 1 = 5, but the first term is 16, so add 11. 5n + 11.
8b ii
nth term = 84 − 9n
Common difference −9 → −9n. −9 × 1 = −9, but the first term is 75, so add 84. −9n + 84 = 84 − 9n.
8c i
10th term = 61
5 × 10 + 11 = 50 + 11 = 61.
8c ii
10th term = −6
84 − 9 × 10 = 84 − 90 = −6.
