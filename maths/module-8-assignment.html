Question 1 — Simplify ratios (3 marks)

Put both parts in the same units first, then divide by the highest common factor.

1a
10 : 3
2 litres = 2000 ml, so 2000 : 600. Divide both by 200 → 10 : 3.
1b
1 : 3
1 day = 24 h, so 8 : 24. Divide both by 8 → 1 : 3.
1c
12 : 1
3 km = 3000 m, so 3000 : 250. Divide both by 250 → 12 : 1.

Question 2 — 6 loaves cost £7.74 (4 marks)

Find the cost of one loaf first (the unitary method).

2a
£11.61
One loaf: 7.74 ÷ 6 = £1.29.
9 loaves: 9 × 1.29 = £11.61.
2b
£5.16
4 loaves: 4 × 1.29 = £5.16.

Question 3 — Simone (30) and Lisa (6) (6 marks)

3a
5 : 1
30 : 6, divide both by 6 → 5 : 1.
3b
3 : 1
In 6 years: Simone 30 + 6 = 36, Lisa 6 + 6 = 12.
36 : 12, divide both by 12 → 3 : 1.
3c
Simone will be 48.
The age gap stays 30 − 6 = 24 years forever. For a ratio of 2 : 1, Simone is twice Lisa, so the gap equals Lisa's age: Lisa = 24, Simone = 2 × 24 = 48.
Check: 48 : 24 = 2 : 1 ✓ (this is 18 years from now).

Question 4 — Cheese blocks (5 marks)

300g = £3.75, 350g = £4.10, 500g = £5.28. Don't round.

4a
£0.125 (12.5p)
One gram of the 300g block: 3.75 ÷ 300 = £0.0125.
10 g: 10 × 0.0125 = £0.125.
4b
The 500g block is best value.
Cost per gram:
300g: 3.75 ÷ 300 = £0.0125
350g: 4.10 ÷ 350 = £0.011714…
500g: 5.28 ÷ 500 = £0.01056
The 500g block is the cheapest per gram, so it is the best value.

Question 5 — Kyle's train tickets (4 marks)

Day £3.50 · 1-week £25 · 4-week £90.

5a
£24.50
7 day-tickets: 7 × 3.50 = £24.50.
5b
The 4-week ticket gives the best value.
Compare cost per day:
Day ticket: £3.50 per day.
1-week: 25 ÷ 7 = £3.57 per day.
4-week: 90 ÷ 28 = £3.21 per day.
The 4-week ticket is the cheapest per day. (Note the 1-week ticket is actually poorer value than buying 7 day-tickets.)
5c
He may not want to pay £90 all at once, or he might not travel every day for the full 4 weeks, so it wouldn't be worth it.
1 mark for any sensible reason — cost up front, holidays, or not travelling enough days to break even.

Question 6 — Feet ↔ metres conversion graph (4 marks)

The line runs from (0, 0) to about (6 feet, 1.8 m), so 1 foot ≈ 0.3 m. Draw a line up from the axis and across to read each value.

6a
≈ 1.2 m
Read up from 4 feet to the line, then across: 4 × 0.3 = 1.2 m.
6b
≈ 2.7 feet
80 cm = 0.8 m. Read across from 0.8 m to the line, then down: 0.8 ÷ 0.3 = 2.67 ≈ 2.7 feet.
6c
5.4 m
6 yards = 6 × 3 = 18 feet. The graph only reaches 6 feet, so scale up: 6 feet = 1.8 m, and 18 feet is 3 × that, so 3 × 1.8 = 5.4 m.

Question 7 — Four-sided spinner, 22 spins (7 marks)

Frequencies: score 1 → 7, score 2 → 4, score 3 → 5, score 4 → 6.

7a
Modal score = 1
Score 1 has the highest frequency (7).
7b
Median = 2.5
With 22 values the median is the average of the 11th and 12th. Counting in order: scores of 1 fill places 1–7, scores of 2 fill places 8–11, scores of 3 fill places 12–16. So the 11th value is 2 and the 12th is 3 → (2 + 3) ÷ 2 = 2.5.
7c
Frequency × Score column: 7, 8, 15, 24; totals — frequency 22, freq × score 54.
1×7 = 7, 2×4 = 8, 3×5 = 15, 4×6 = 24. Sum = 7 + 8 + 15 + 24 = 54.
7d
Mean ≈ 2.45
Mean = Σ(f × score) ÷ Σf = 54 ÷ 22 = 2.454… ≈ 2.45.

Question 9 — Draw a pie chart of exam responses (4 marks)

90 students: Easy 22, OK 37, Hard 19, No reply 12. Angle = (frequency ÷ 90) × 360° = frequency × 4°.

9
Easy 88° · OK 148° · Hard 76° · No reply 48°
Since 360 ÷ 90 = 4, multiply each frequency by 4:
Easy 22 × 4 = 88°; OK 37 × 4 = 148°; Hard 19 × 4 = 76°; No reply 12 × 4 = 48°.
Check they add to a full circle: 88 + 148 + 76 + 48 = 360° ✓. Then draw each slice with a protractor.
