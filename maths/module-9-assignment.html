Question 1 — The percentages trick (6 marks)

The trick from 9.1: x% of y = y% of x. Swap them round so the percentage becomes something friendly (50%, 25%, 10% or 1%). Each part needs both the swap shown and the answer worked out.

1a
2
4% of 50 = 50% of 4
50% is just half, so 50% of 4 = 4 ÷ 2 = 2.
1b
20
80% of 25 = 25% of 80
25% is a quarter, so 25% of 80 = 80 ÷ 4 = 20.
1c
23
230% of 10 = 10% of 230
10% is a tenth, so 10% of 230 = 230 ÷ 10 = 23.
The trick still works with a percentage over 100 — swapping turns an awkward 230% into an easy 10%.

Question 2 — Car boot sale, £88 (6 marks)

The takings are £88 and each girl gets 25%. Both methods must be shown, then say which is easier.

2
Both girls get £22. Sally's method is the easier one.
Sally — 25% of £88:
25% is a quarter, so halve and halve again:
88 ÷ 2 = 44, then 44 ÷ 2 = 22 → £22

Jenna — 88% of £25:
10% of 25 = 2.50, so 80% = 8 × 2.50 = 20
1% of 25 = 0.25, so 8% = 8 × 0.25 = 2
80% + 8% = 20 + 2 = £22

Both give £22, as they must — 25% of 88 = 88% of 25. But Sally's is easier: two halvings in one step, against building 88% up from 80% and 8%. So this is a case where swapping round doesn't help — the trick is only worth using when the swap gives you the friendlier percentage.

Question 3 — Match each decimal to its fraction (6 marks)

One mark per correct line. The method from 9.2: a one-digit repeating block gives a denominator of 9, a two-digit block gives 99, a three-digit block gives 999. Decimals that stop are just tenths or hundredths.

DecimalFractionWhy
0.3333333…1/3One repeating digit → 9x = 3, so x = 3/9 = 1/3
0.323232323…32/99Two-digit block "32" → 99x = 32
0.33/10Terminates after one decimal place → 3 tenths
0.323323323…323/999Three-digit block "323" → 999x = 323
0.328/25Terminates → 32/100, divide both by 4 → 8/25
0.232323…23/99Two-digit block "23" → 99x = 23
Watch out
0.323232… and 0.323323323… look almost identical. Read the block that actually repeats: the first repeats 32 (→ 99), the second repeats 323 (→ 999). Likewise 0.3 and 0.32 stop — they are not recurring at all.

Question 4 — Time series graph, library issues (5 marks)

Issues in hundreds of thousands, 2001–2011:

Year20012002200320042005200620072008200920102011
Issues125131122118125129132135128123120
4
Years along the horizontal axis; vertical axis running from 115 to 140, not from 0.
The hint is the important bit. All the values sit between 118 and 135, so starting the vertical axis at 0 squashes the whole graph into a thin band at the top. Instead:

Horizontal axis: Year, 2001 to 2011 (one square per year)
Vertical axis: Issues (hundreds of thousands), 115 to 140 in steps of 5

Put a zig-zag (break) symbol on the vertical axis to show it doesn't start at zero. Plot the eleven points, join them with straight lines drawn with a ruler, and label both axes plus a title.

The marks are for: a sensible restricted scale, both axes labelled, all points plotted correctly, and the points joined with straight lines.

Shape to check against: falls to a low of 118 in 2004, climbs to a peak of 135 in 2008, then falls away to 120 by 2011.

Question 5 — Rectangular-based pyramid (3 marks)

ABCDE has a rectangular base 12 by 8, with the apex E directly above the centre F.

5a
2 planes of symmetry
Each plane is vertical, passes through the apex E, and cuts through the midpoints of a pair of opposite base edges — one running the length of the rectangle, one across the width.
5b
4 planes of symmetry. The extra two are the diagonal ones, which only work because the base is a square.
The vertical planes of symmetry of a pyramid line up with the lines of symmetry of its base:

Square: 4 lines of symmetry → 4 planes
Rectangle: 2 lines of symmetry → 2 planes

A square has two lines through the midpoints of opposite sides and two along its diagonals. A rectangle has only the first two — its diagonals are not lines of symmetry, because folding along a diagonal doesn't match the long side onto the short side. So the rectangular pyramid loses the two diagonal planes and is left with 2.

Question 7 — Park visitors over a year (5 marks)

Read off the graph: Jan 500, Feb 600, Mar 750, Apr 800, May 900, Jun 1200, Jul 1500, Aug 1400, Sep 1100, Oct 900, Nov 700, Dec 600.

7a
July
The highest point on the graph, at about 1500 visitors.
7b
Visitor numbers rise steadily from January to a peak in July, then fall steadily back down to December.
Describe the whole year in one sentence, not individual points. Mentioning that it's a seasonal pattern — busiest in summer, quietest in winter — is the ideal answer.
7c
The biggest drop is August to September (1400 → 1100, a fall of 300), and numbers keep falling through October and November. Likely because the school summer holidays end and the autumn weather turns colder and wetter with shorter days.
1 mark for identifying the month(s), 1 mark for a sensible reason. Any reasonable explanation earns the second mark — back to school, worse weather, darker evenings, holidays over.
7d
About 500 visitors
December is at 600 and still falling. The pattern repeats each year, so January should come back to roughly the same value as the January shown — about 500. Anything from about 450 to 550 with a reason is fine.

Question 8 — Stem-and-leaf, ages on a bus (6 marks)

Key 2|5 = 25 years old. Written out in order, the 30 ages are:

2, 3, 4, 5
13, 13, 15, 18, 18
20, 23, 24, 24, 24, 25, 25, 25, 26, 27, 29
31, 33, 37
42, 48, 49
60
70, 76, 77

8a
Median = 25 years
There are 4 + 5 + 11 + 3 + 3 + 0 + 1 + 3 = 30 people. With an even number of values the median is the mean of the 15th and 16th.
Counting along the rows: the 0s fill places 1–4, the 10s fill places 5–9, and the 20s fill places 10–20. So both the 15th and 16th values are in the 20s row: 20, 23, 24, 24, 24, 25, 25, …
Median = (25 + 25) ÷ 2 = 25.
8b
Range = 75 years · Modes = 24 and 25
Range = largest − smallest = 77 − 2 = 75.
For the mode, look for the most repeated leaf values: 24 appears three times and 25 appears three times, and nothing else appears more than twice. So the data is bimodal — both 24 and 25 are modes. (The question's "mode(s)" is the hint that there is more than one.)
8c
Mean ≈ 29.5 years
Calculator allowed here. Add each row, then total:
0s: 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 = 14
10s: 13 + 13 + 15 + 18 + 18 = 77
20s: 20 + 23 + 24 + 24 + 24 + 25 + 25 + 25 + 26 + 27 + 29 = 272
30s: 31 + 33 + 37 = 101
40s: 42 + 48 + 49 = 139
60s: 60
70s: 70 + 76 + 77 = 223
Total = 14 + 77 + 272 + 101 + 139 + 60 + 223 = 886
Mean = 886 ÷ 30 = 29.53… ≈ 29.5 years.

Worth a sanity check: the mean (29.5) is higher than the median (25), pulled up by the few people in their 60s and 70s.
