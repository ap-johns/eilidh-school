Question 1 — Solve by balancing, then check (9 marks)

Do the same operation to both sides until the letter is alone, then substitute the answer back into the original equation to check.

1a
u = 5
u + 7 = 12
u = 12 − 7   (subtract 7 from both sides)
u = 5
Check: 5 + 7 = 12 ✓
1b
v = 7
2v − 5 = 9
2v = 14   (add 5 to both sides)
v = 7   (divide both sides by 2)
Check: 2 × 7 − 5 = 14 − 5 = 9 ✓
1c
w = 7
3w − 3 = 2w + 4
w − 3 = 4   (subtract 2w from both sides)
w = 7   (add 3 to both sides)
Check: LHS 3 × 7 − 3 = 18; RHS 2 × 7 + 4 = 18 ✓

Question 2 — Expand brackets, solve, then check (12 marks)

Multiply out the bracket first, then balance both sides. Check each answer in the original bracketed equation.

2a
h = 2
2(h + 1) = 6
2h + 2 = 6   (expand)
2h = 4   (subtract 2)
h = 2   (divide by 2)
Check: 2(2 + 1) = 2 × 3 = 6 ✓
2b
j = −50
9(j + 5) = 8j − 5
9j + 45 = 8j − 5   (expand)
j + 45 = −5   (subtract 8j)
j = −50   (subtract 45)
Check: LHS 9(−50 + 5) = 9 × (−45) = −405; RHS 8 × (−50) − 5 = −405 ✓
2c
k = 9
2(k − 3) = 4(k − 6)
2k − 6 = 4k − 24   (expand both brackets)
18 = 2k   (add 24, subtract 2k)
k = 9   (divide by 2)
Check: LHS 2(9 − 3) = 2 × 6 = 12; RHS 4(9 − 6) = 4 × 3 = 12 ✓

Question 3 — True or false (2 marks)

3a
True
A rhombus is a parallelogram (with 4 equal sides), and in any parallelogram the opposite angles are equal.
3b
False
An isosceles trapezium has two pairs of equal angles — the two base angles at the bottom are equal, and the two angles at the top are equal.

Question 4 — Match each shape to its description (6 marks)

4
4 right angles, 4 equal sides → square
4 right angles, 2 pairs of equal opposite sides → rectangle
2 pairs of adjacent sides, 1 pair of equal angles, 1 reflex angle → arrowhead
2 pairs of adjacent sides, 1 pair of equal angles, no reflex angle → kite
Looks like a pushed over rectangle → parallelogram
Exactly one pair of parallel sides → trapezium

Question 5 — Area of shapes (4 marks)

Use the perpendicular (vertical) height, not the slanted side.

5a
80 mm²
Parallelogram: A = base × height.
A = 10 × 8 = 80 mm²   (base 10 mm, perpendicular height 8 mm)
5b
28 cm²
Trapezium: A = ½ × (a + b) × h, where a and b are the parallel sides.
A = ½ × (5 + 9) × 4 = ½ × 14 × 4 = 28 cm²

Question 6 — Parallelogram split into two trapeziums (7 marks)

The parallelogram has base 8 cm and perpendicular height 5 cm. A slanted line splits it into trapezium A (left) and trapezium B (right). The bottom edge is split 3 cm / 5 cm and the top edge is split 6 cm / 2 cm.

6a
40 cm²
Parallelogram: A = base × height = 8 × 5 = 40 cm².
6b
Trapezium A = 22.5 cm²; Trapezium B = 17.5 cm²
Each trapezium has the two horizontal edges as its parallel sides and a height of 5 cm.
A — parallel sides 3 cm (bottom) and 6 cm (top, = 8 − 2):
A = ½ × (3 + 6) × 5 = ½ × 9 × 5 = 22.5 cm²
B — parallel sides 2 cm (top) and 5 cm (bottom, = 8 − 3):
A = ½ × (2 + 5) × 5 = ½ × 7 × 5 = 17.5 cm²
6c
22.5 + 17.5 = 40 cm², which matches part a.
Adding the two trapezium areas gives the parallelogram area. This works because the two trapeziums together make up the whole parallelogram — cutting it with the slanted line moves no area and adds none, so the two pieces must add back to the original 40 cm².
