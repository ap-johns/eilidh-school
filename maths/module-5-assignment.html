Question 1 — Expand brackets (5 marks)

Multiply everything inside the bracket by what's outside. In part c, expand both brackets then collect like terms.

1a
18q + 27
9 × 2q = 18q; 9 × 3 = 27.
1b
2r² − 4r
r × 2r = 2r²; r × (−4) = −4r.
1c
2s + 5
Expand each bracket — mind the minus on the second one:
3(2s − 1) = 6s − 3
−2(2s − 4) = −4s + 8
Collect like terms: 6s − 4s = 2s; −3 + 8 = 5.

Question 2 — Factorise (5 marks)

Pull out the highest common factor (HCF) of the numbers and any letters common to every term. Check by expanding back.

2a
7(6a − b)
HCF of 42 and 7 is 7. 42a ÷ 7 = 6a; 7b ÷ 7 = b.
2b
3xy(2x − 9)
HCF of 6 and 27 is 3. Both terms contain x and y, so take out 3xy.
6x²y ÷ 3xy = 2x; 27xy ÷ 3xy = 9.
2c
3x(10x² + 4y − 7z)
HCF of 30, 12 and 21 is 3. Every term contains x (but not y or z), so take out 3x.
30x³ ÷ 3x = 10x²; 12xy ÷ 3x = 4y; 21xz ÷ 3x = 7z.

Question 3 — Make a the subject (8 marks)

Same operation on both sides, working backwards through the steps. Show each line.

3a
a = (c − 11) / 3
c = 3a + 11
c − 11 = 3a   (subtract 11)
a = (c − 11) / 3   (divide by 3)
3b
a = (c + 9) / 4
c = 4a − 9
c + 9 = 4a   (add 9)
a = (c + 9) / 4   (divide by 4)
3c
a = 4(c + 5)   (or 4c + 20)
c = a/4 − 5
c + 5 = a/4   (add 5)
a = 4(c + 5)   (multiply by 4)
3d
a = 9c − 2
c = (a + 2) / 9
9c = a + 2   (multiply by 9)
a = 9c − 2   (subtract 2)

Question 6 — Steve and Sally's circle, diameter 5 m (3 marks)

6a
Steve used the diameter as the radius; Sally squared the whole product instead of just the radius.
Steve: he put r = 5, but 5 m is the diameter. He should have halved it first to get r = 2.5 m.
Sally: she used the correct radius 2.5, but squared the whole π × 2.5 (getting 7.853…²). The square applies only to the radius — she should square 2.5 first, then multiply by π.
6b
≈ 19.6 m²
Radius = 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5 m.
A = πr² = π × 2.5² = π × 6.25 = 19.635 ≈ 19.6 m².

Question 7 — Is the area doubled? (3 marks)

Amrit thinks a circle of radius 4 m has double the area of one with radius 2 m. Find both areas to check.

7
No — it is four times as big, not double.
Radius 4: A = π × 4² = 16π ≈ 50.3 m².
Radius 2: A = π × 2² = 4π ≈ 12.6 m².
50.3 ÷ 12.6 = 4, so the larger circle is four times the area.
This is because area depends on the radius squared: doubling the radius multiplies the area by 2² = 4.

Question 8 — Semi-circle, radius 5 cm (6 marks)

A semi-circle is half a circle. The perimeter is half the circumference plus the straight diameter across the bottom.

8 · Area
≈ 39.3 cm²
Half the area of the full circle:
A = ½ × πr² = ½ × π × 5² = ½ × 25π = 12.5π
= 39.27 ≈ 39.3 cm².
8 · Perimeter
≈ 25.7 cm
Half the circumference plus the diameter:
Curved part = ½ × 2πr = πr = π × 5 = 15.708 cm.
Diameter = 2 × 5 = 10 cm.
Perimeter = 15.708 + 10 = 25.708 ≈ 25.7 cm.

Question 9 — Area 114 cm², find the circumference (3 marks)

Work backwards: use the area to find the radius, then use the radius to find the circumference.

9
≈ 37.8 cm
From A = πr²: r² = A ÷ π = 114 ÷ π = 36.285.
r = √36.285 = 6.024 cm.
Then C = 2πr = 2 × π × 6.024 = 37.85 ≈ 37.8 cm.
