Question 1 — Angles between parallel lines (8 marks)

Two horizontal parallel lines are crossed by two transversals that meet at a point on the lower line. 150° is marked at the top-left intersection (obtuse, on the left); 50° is marked above the top line at the top-right intersection.

a
a = 30°
The 150° at the top-left intersection and the 30° (= 180° − 150°) on the same straight line are angles on a straight line. The 30° interior angle of the triangle and angle a are alternate angles (between the parallel lines, opposite sides of the left transversal), so a = 30°.
b
b = 50°
b is vertically opposite the 50° marked above the top line, so b = 50°.
c
c = 50°
c and b are alternate angles across the right transversal between the two parallel lines, so c = b = 50°.
d
d = 100°
a, d and c are angles on a straight line (the bottom parallel line) above it: a + d + c = 180°.
30° + d + 50° = 180° → d = 100°.
Or: angles in the triangle sum to 180° — interior angles 30° + 50° + d = 180°.

Question 3 — Regular polygon (4 marks)

3
Exterior angle = 8°. Number of sides = 45.
Interior + exterior = 180° (angles on a straight line at each vertex).
Exterior = 180° − 172° = 8°.
For a regular polygon: exterior × n = 360°.
n = 360 ÷ 8 = 45 sides.

Question 4 — Quadrilateral angle (5 marks)

4a
f = 130°
Angles in a quadrilateral sum to 360°.
f = 360° − 100° − 68° − 62° = 130°.
4b
Construction task — see method.
1. Measure each side of the printed quadrilateral with a ruler and write the lengths on the diagram.
2. On fresh paper, draw the bottom side accurately.
3. Use a protractor at each end to draw rays at the given angles (68° at the bottom-left, 62° at the bottom-right).
4. Measure along each ray to mark the next vertex at the correct distance.
5. Join the two upper vertices to complete the quadrilateral.
Check: the angle at the top-left should measure 100° (and the top-right 130°).

Question 5 — Map scale (3 marks)

5
16 cm on the map
Convert real distance to cm:
40 km = 40 × 1000 × 100 = 4 000 000 cm.
Map distance = real ÷ scale factor = 4 000 000 ÷ 250 000 = 16 cm.

Question 6 — Three towns (6 marks)

Diagram: Alton (A) at the bottom with a North line; Bolton (B) is to the upper-right with AB = 6 cm at 46° from North (clockwise); Carlton (C) is to the upper-left at 62° from North (anticlockwise). Scale: 1 cm : 2.5 km.

6a
Alton to Bolton = 15 km
AB on the diagram = 6 cm. Real distance = 6 × 2.5 = 15 km.
6bi
Mark the angle at B, measured clockwise from B's North line round to BA (the line going down-left back to Alton).
6bii
Bearing of Alton from Bolton = 226°
Forward bearing of B from A = 046°.
Back bearing = forward + 180° (since forward < 180°).
= 046° + 180° = 226°.
6ci
Mark the angle at C, measured clockwise from C's North line round to CA (the line going down-right back to Alton).
6cii
Bearing of Alton from Carlton = 118°
Forward bearing of C from A: C is 62° anticlockwise of N at A, so the bearing of C from A = 360° − 62° = 298°.
Back bearing = forward − 180° (since forward > 180°).
= 298° − 180° = 118°.

Question 7 — Boat journey (4 marks)

7
Construction task — method below.
Scale: 1 cm = 1 km.
1. Mark point A. Draw a faint North line straight up from A.
2. Use a protractor: measure 135° clockwise from A's North line and draw a ray.
3. Along that ray, measure 7 cm and mark point B.
4. At B, draw a new (parallel) North line straight up from B.
5. Measure 250° clockwise from B's North line — i.e. into the south-west region — and draw a ray.
6. Along that ray, measure 12 cm and mark point C.
Check: 135° points roughly south-east; 250° points roughly south-west.

Question 8 — Triangle PQR (4 marks)

Given: PQ = 65 m (= 6.5 cm at 1 cm : 10 m), angle P = 42°, angle Q = 69°. Type: ASA.

8a
Construction task.
1. Draw PQ = 6.5 cm.
2. At P, use a protractor to draw a ray at 42° above PQ.
3. At Q, draw a ray at 69° above PQ.
4. Where the two rays meet is R.
8b
Angle R = 69°
Angles in a triangle sum to 180°: R = 180° − 42° − 69° = 69°. Measured value should be within ±2°.
8c
Isosceles triangle
Two angles are equal (Q = R = 69°), so the two sides opposite them are equal (PR = PQ). A triangle with two equal sides / two equal angles is isosceles.

Question 9 — Triangle SUT (4 marks)

Given: ST = 6 cm (= 60 m), SU = 4 cm (= 40 m), angle S = 45°. Type: SAS.

9a
Construction task.
1. Draw the base ST = 6 cm.
2. At S, use a protractor to draw a ray at 45° above ST.
3. Measure 4 cm along that ray and mark U.
4. Join U to T.
9b
Angle U ≈ 93°
Measured with a protractor on the construction. Accept 91–95°.
9c
Use the angle-sum-of-a-triangle rule.
Angles in a triangle sum to 180°.
T = 180° − S − U = 180° − 45° − 93° ≈ 42°.
